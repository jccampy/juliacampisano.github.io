02 / The Problem

Marketing couldn't engage customers the way they wanted.

Our customer communications were spread across multiple platforms. Some communications came through our loyalty provider, others through SendGrid, and others through middleware.

That fragmentation created operational complexity. Teams had to know which system powered each type of communication, and onboarding new team members meant teaching multiple platforms and processes.

Marketing also couldn't consistently use the same customer target groups across communication channels, limiting their ability to create connected customer journeys.

Certain customer-facing content created an even larger bottleneck. Updating some promotional smart tiles could require development work and take months to change.

Product Challenge

How might we centralize customer engagement while giving Marketing better targeting, greater flexibility, and less dependency on development?

06 / Product Decisions

Treat the platform like a product, not a tool.

A large portion of the product work involved deciding how quickly to expand the platform, where self-service made sense, and when business requests needed to be adjusted because of technical constraints.

01

Phase around incremental value

I prioritized everyday communication capabilities first so Marketing could gain value within the first few months rather than waiting for one large final launch.

02

Remove unnecessary development work

Braze-powered For You tiles allowed Marketing to update content immediately rather than waiting potentially months for a development release.

03

Balance business asks with technical reality

I worked closely with developers to understand what Braze could realistically support and pushed back when stakeholder requests did not make technical sense or introduced unnecessary complexity.

04

Learn while building

Product and Engineering were learning Braze together. We used ongoing technical discovery to adjust requirements and the roadmap as we uncovered new platform capabilities and limitations.

05

Build reusable capabilities

The goal was not to solve individual campaigns. We built capabilities and placements Marketing could reuse across future customer journeys.

06

Know when the platform may not be the answer

Geofencing introduced persistent technical challenges. Based on what we've learned, we continue to evaluate whether Braze is the right long-term solution for that specific use case.

07 / Customer Experience

08 / Technical Considerations

Customer engagement depended on the systems behind it.

Braze required more than adding a new marketing platform. The implementation touched identity, customer data, segmentation, SDKs, mobile permissions, and ongoing platform maintenance.

Identity

Loyalty ID → Braze Customer ID

We linked loyalty program user IDs to the corresponding Braze customer identity so engagement and customer data stayed connected to the correct user.

Data

Rebuild segmentation

Existing customer segments needed to be reconsidered and recreated using the user data available within the new ecosystem.

SDK

Ongoing platform maintenance

The implementation required SDK changes across our digital products. New Braze versions and capabilities continue to require evaluation, development, and upgrades.

Location

A new permission + adoption challenge

Geofencing required asking customers for location permissions for marketing purposes, something we had not historically done. Adoption and platform reliability remain areas we continue to evaluate.

10 / Testing + UAT

Testing the product meant testing the configuration too.

A technically correct implementation could still result in the wrong customer experience if a campaign was configured incorrectly. That meant our testing process had to extend beyond traditional development QA.

Product

Requirements

Confirm the experience behaved according to the intended business and customer requirements.

Engineering

Technical QA

Validate SDK behavior, integrations, placements, and development implementation.

Marketing

Campaign Setup

Confirm campaigns, audience rules, content, and Braze configuration were set up correctly.

Implementation Partner

Platform Validation

Troubleshoot Braze-specific behavior, platform configuration, and implementation issues.

What Changed

Early releases exposed gaps between technical QA and Marketing configuration. I evolved the UAT process to bring Product, Marketing, Development, and our implementation partner together earlier so we could validate the entire customer experience before release.

12 / What I Learned

The product is bigger than the platform.

This implementation reinforced that launching technology is only one part of building a successful platform. The processes, users, integrations, operating model, and customer experience around it matter just as much.

01

Platform implementations are product work.

Success isn't simply getting software live. The platform has to fit the people, processes, customer experience, data, and systems around it.

02

Technical constraints belong in the product conversation.

Working closely with Engineering helped me challenge requests that didn't make technical sense and find better tradeoffs with stakeholders.

03

New capabilities change operating processes too.

Marketing configuration became part of the customer experience, which meant training, governance, and UAT were just as important as development.

04

A vendor solution isn't automatically the right solution forever.

Geofencing reinforced the importance of evaluating whether a platform continues to solve the underlying problem rather than forcing every use case into the same technology.
