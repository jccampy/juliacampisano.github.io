Loyalty Tier Experience.

01 / The Problem

Changing the rules without losing the customer.

Historically, RaceTrac loyalty tiers did not expire. Once a customer reached the highest tier, they could retain that status indefinitely, regardless of whether their purchasing behavior continued to meet the requirements.

This created a business challenge. Some of our highest-tier customers were primarily visiting RaceTrac to redeem their tier benefit, particularly their free dispensed beverage, without making additional purchases.

At the same time, RaceTrac was introducing a new tier structure and updated point values. Customers would now need to actively earn points to achieve their status for the following year.

That meant we weren't simply launching a new feature. We were changing an established customer expectation.

The Product Challenge

How do we introduce a significant change to a loyalty program without making customers feel like we're taking something away?

02 / Business Requirements

Make the new program achievable, valuable, and understandable.

The business needed the new loyalty structure to reward meaningful engagement while giving customers a clear path toward maintaining or reaching their desired tier.

01

02

Meaningful Engagement

Ensure tier benefits were connected to continued customer spending and engagement.

03

Customers would need to earn enough points during the qualification period to achieve their tier for the following year.

04

Clear Communication

Explain a significant program change across mobile and web without overwhelming customers.

05

Customer Retention

Maintain engagement during a moment where customers could perceive the change as losing a benefit.

06

Progress Visibility

Give customers a clear way to understand their current status, what they are working toward, and how close they are to their next tier.

03 / Customer Need

Clarity first. Then motivation.

CUSTOMER NEED / 01

Understand the change.

Customers needed a straightforward explanation of what was changing, why their tier was changing, how the new point structure worked, and what they needed to earn.

CUSTOMER NEED / 02

Feel excited about what's next.

Simply communicating that customers were losing their previous status risked creating frustration. We wanted the experience to make the new structure feel achievable and motivating.

05 / Product Decisions

Turning status into a reason to come back.

DECISION / 01

Make progress the centerpiece.

I envisioned a new Status experience that would become the customer's destination for understanding their loyalty position. Instead of simply showing a tier name, the experience would help customers understand what they were working toward and how close they were.

DECISION / 02

Turn a difficult change into a goal.

Customers were potentially losing a benefit they had historically received indefinitely. Rather than making the experience solely about what was changing, I focused the experience around what customers could earn next.

DECISION / 03

Make the new structure understandable.

The new tier structure and point values introduced additional complexity. The digital experience needed to visually connect points, tiers, progress, and benefits so customers could understand the relationship between them.

DECISION / 04

Design for repeat engagement.

The Status experience wasn't intended to be a one-time information page. It was designed as a destination customers could return to throughout the year to check their progress and understand what they were working toward.

07 / Technical Considerations

The experience was only as good as the data behind it.

Loyalty Provider Constraints

Our loyalty provider did not fully support the tier reset functionality required for the program change, creating additional operational and technical complexity.

Data Dependencies

We relied heavily on our data team to support customer tier updates and validate the results of the reset.

Multiple Customer Scenarios

We needed to test different customer types and tier levels to ensure the correct status and experience appeared.

Cross-System Validation

The customer's new status needed to be reflected correctly across the relevant digital experiences and supporting systems.

10 / What I Learned

Product changes don't happen in a vacuum.

LEARNING / 01

Change management is product work.

A major change to an existing customer experience requires as much thought about customer perception as it does functionality.

LEARNING / 02

Constraints shape the product.

Platform limitations, data dependencies, and operational constraints need to be considered early rather than treated as problems at launch.

LEARNING / 03

Progress can be a product feature.

Turning a static loyalty status into a visible journey gave customers a reason to return, engage, and work toward something.
